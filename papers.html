Manuscript 1 · foreign-key discovery

RefSel: Risk-Controlled Foreign-Key Discovery with Bounded Language-Model Evidence

Jiamao Lin, Weisong Sun*
* Corresponding author · Nanyang Technological University

IEEE TKDE · manuscript in preparation for submission

A column can fit perfectly inside several integer keys and still tell us very little about which table it references. RefSel turns raw tables into candidate keys, scores their context, and decodes a consistent schema. Optional language-model evidence has a bounded role; a separate calibration step decides which discovered keys to retain.

The problem: inclusion is not a reference

Across 259 public databases, 80% of declared foreign keys target integer keys; 64% of those have a competing key that also contains every source value. Dense counters create accidental matches, while genuinely indistinguishable targets impose a limit on what values alone can identify.

The contribution: from local scores to a whole schema

A hypergeometric chance model measures inclusion significance. Two-pass relational scoring describes each candidate and its rivals; CP-SAT coordinates key choices across the database. Confidence shares probability among competing targets, and Learn-then-Test controls the expected false-discovery proportion of a retained subset.

01.1The method, in equations

RefSel · Section 3
  1. STAGE 1Generate

    Find minimal unique column combinations; keep high-coverage semi-join candidates.

  2. STAGE 2Score

    Combine chance inclusion, source-group competition and relational context.

  3. STAGE 3Decode

    Select keys and references together under schema constraints.

  4. STAGE 4Share confidence

    Separate “a reference exists” from “this is the right target.”

  5. STAGE 5Calibrate & retain

    Choose a threshold on independent units, or abstain.

01How surprising is the overlap?

PrH0(X=i)=(Ni)(u−Nk−i)(uk)

The source has k distinct values, the target N, and the unrelated domain size is u. The score se = −log PrH₀(X ≥ m) measures the surprise of at least m matches. Conservative domain estimates prevent dense integer counters from looking artificially convincing.

02Bound the language-model contribution

teL=te+Bϖe,|ϖe|≤1

The prior can shift a queried candidate’s logit by at most B = 6. The exact decoder still enforces schema constraints; a second LLM pass can veto decoded edges. The frozen independent rounds query every source group; selective querying is used in the development/external setting.

03Choose a consistent schema jointly

max(x,y)∈C(D)∑T,κaTκxTκ+∑eteye

x selects a key (or none) for each table; y selects foreign keys. Constraints coordinate the target key, permit at most one target per source group, and exclude a mutual reverse pair. General cycles and self-references remain feasible. CP-SAT solves the whole database jointly.

04Share confidence among rivals

ce=π^g·ete∑f∈getf

Being sure a column references something does not identify its target. With a reference probability of 0.99 and two tied candidates, each gets 0.495. This motivates the ambiguity explorer below; calibration of the reference probability alone does not establish calibration of the product.

05Retain only a calibrated subset

Pr(R(τ^)≤α)≥1−δ

FDP = false retained keys / max(1, retained keys). Learn-then-Test chooses a threshold using independent calibration units; R is the expected unit-mean FDP. The predictor and threshold grid must be frozen and calibration/test units must follow the same distribution. This is not a bound for each individual database.

Definitions and assumptions: Section 3, including the identifiability and retention propositions. Stage 1 normally retains coverage ≥ 0.9, with explicitly defined low-cardinality and constant-source exceptions.

01.2What the experiments show

Frozen independent evaluation

The strongest transferable result is the value of bounded language-model evidence. On 60 unseen databases, adding the prior and verifier raises RefSel from 0.402 to 0.476 macro F1. The conventional-schema subset improves more; noisy WikiDBs snapshots are limited by candidate coverage and non-unique target labels.

01.3How far the conclusion goes

Internal accuracy is not independent transfer

0.897 is the named BIRD/Spider cross-validation result. The separate 110-database test collection spans three frozen rounds. Stage-2 gains over RefSel-base are not significant on every new collection.

Risk control needs a matching calibration population

At α = 0.20, all reported lines in the three independent rounds stay below target. One Round-2 anonymous line reaches 0.122 at α = 0.10. Sparse ERP samples and synthetic distractors can break control transferred from clean data.

The registered retention endpoint remains unresolved

P2 specified RefSel-base with prior + verifier, but the saved retention report used edges decoded without the prior. The 0.55 → 1.97 retained-key contrast is supplementary and does not establish that registered endpoint.

A missing candidate cannot be recovered downstream

Empty tables, non-unique target keys and small seed datasets constrain value-based discovery. Declared-key labels can also miss genuine undeclared references. Report the ground-truth denominator alongside F1.

Manuscript 2 · relational-to-ontology learning

What Matters in Relational-to-Ontology Learning?
Data Evidence, Completeness Guard, and Scoring

Jiamao Lin, Weisong Sun*

IEEE TKDE · manuscript in preparation for submission

Once prompts and outputs are complete, a larger model is not automatically a better ontology modeller. This study combines a zero-call data-profiling route with an LLM route and asks where each helps. The revised manuscript adds selective completeness guarding, tests the boundary created by missing keys, and measures mappings by the instance tuples they actually produce.

Data evidence provides a strong starting point

Det-v2 profiles unique column combinations, inclusion and functional dependencies, table roles, and value partitions. It builds classes, attributes and relations with no model call. With declared keys, its eight-database mean is 0.611, compared with 0.637–0.640 for the strongest whole-schema LLM configurations.

Completeness and scoring change the story

A table omitted from a prompt or truncated out of a response cannot contribute a complete mapping. Yet calling a model once per table repeats context and can change modelling conventions. A selective guard repairs omissions; an extent-based evaluator checks what the resulting ontology denotes.

02.1Four components you can inspect

Methods & measurement · Sections IV–V

01 · Det-v2: profile the data

Minimal approximate UCCs identify keys, high-coverage INDs suggest joins, and FD/value-partition rules suggest concept structure. Type-like discriminators and lexical cues constrain rule firing. TPC-H sf30 (2.6 × 10⁸ rows) runs end to end in 14 minutes at 26.8 GB peak RAM in the reported setup.

02 · Completeness guard: query only omissions

Start with a single whole-schema call, audit table coverage, and merge missing-table fragments behind the original mapping. Recovering a missing table should not overwrite an already coherent global choice.

03 · Cert: calibrate cross-source agreement

Pool unique elements, count distinct system arms and repeated samples, then calibrate a threshold at the database level. The theoretical risk bound depends on exchangeability; cross-benchmark agreement alone cannot supply it.

04 · Extent metric: execute before comparing

Compare the actual instance tuples produced by each mapping. Ordered relation endpoints make inverse relations distinguishable; one-to-one matching prevents multiple predictions from claiming the same gold element.

01Find omitted tables before making more calls

Tmiss=T(S)∖tbl(MWD)

A single whole-schema call produces MWD. The guard checks which schema tables are mentioned by any mapping element, then requests only the omitted tables. A link table represented as a relation already counts as covered.

02Measure agreement across sources

a(e)=narm(e)+12nsamp(e)SLLM

narm counts proposing system arms; nsamp counts proposing LLM samples. Cert thresholds this score using database-level Learn-then-Test. Agreement is useful for ranking review effort, but shared model errors do not become independent evidence.

03Let the whole-schema model win every conflict

Mguard=MWD∪{m∈MPT(Tmiss)∣κ(m)∉κ(MWD)}

The equivalence key κ identifies mapping elements. Fallback fragments fill omissions while preserving the original global modelling choices. The reported 1.12× guard is the minimal unmentioned-table version; stricter guards are separate variants.

04Score what a mapping actually denotes

J(e,g)=|⟦e⟧I∩⟦g⟧I||⟦e⟧I∪⟦g⟧I|,J(e,g)≥τ

Execute predicted element e and gold element g on data I. Classes yield key tuples, relations ordered subject–object pairs, and attributes subject–literal pairs. Maximum-weight one-to-one matching uses τ = 1 in the reported strict evaluation, making relation direction observable.

Source: Sections IV–V, manuscript Equations (1)–(3). The missing-table definition is shown separately here for readability.

When declared keys are removed

0.35 vs. 0.53–0.60

On the pre-registered @stripped eight-database test, Det-v2 relation F1 falls from 0.56 to 0.35. Four LLM configurations retain 0.53–0.60, a +0.18 to +0.25 advantage (Holm p ≤ 0.005). This is relation F1, not the three-way mean above.

When relation direction is actually scored

−0.37 to −0.46

Instance-extent scoring lowers rule-system held-out relation F1 by 0.37–0.46 relative to direction-blind mapping scores; the LLM drop is about 0.12. The metric changes which systems lead on relations.

Model scale is a conditional result. Across seven non-NPD held-out schemas, 13 whole-schema configurations span 0.595–0.627 three-way F1. Including the 70-table NPD schema makes output completion a major source of separation. The 9,673-output census finds incompleteness rising from 0.2% on 1–5 tables to 27% on 51–100 tables.

02.3Interpretation, protocol and limits

Keep the experimental tracks separate

The main study uses sample rows; the paired experiment uses complete schema-only prompts and three models. Hybrid and Cert sources were not rerun under that paired protocol. The paper’s title now reflects this broader set of experiments.

Small numbers of databases limit general claims

Eight data-bearing held-out databases, including three generated databases, are the effective independent units. The guard fires on four calls. Non-significant differences do not demonstrate equivalence or universal model invariance.

Published baselines are not all rerun comparisons

Burr’s published Gemini 2.5 Pro values precede constraint corrections, while these runs and corrected Ontop/RDB2Onto rows use the corrected release. Baseline gains should retain that version qualification.

Agreement is not a guarantee under distribution shift

Cert controls risk under development calibration, but transferred thresholds exceed the target on real schemas. Shared convention errors break the calibration assumptions. Extent execution also finds 12 empty-extent gold defects; conversion checks establish agreement only on the available snapshots.

Scorer checks: zero disagreement on 640 main-study units and 222 paired mappings. Further protocol, conversion, licensing and reproducibility details: extended results.
